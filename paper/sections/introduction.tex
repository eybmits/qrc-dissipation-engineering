\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Reservoir computing processes temporal information with a fixed dynamical
system and trains only a linear readout~\cite{Maass2002,JaegerESP2001}.
Quantum reservoir computing (QRC) applies this principle to quantum
dynamics~\cite{Fujii2017} and has been pursued in platforms including spin
networks, superconducting processors, fermionic and bosonic modes, and photonic
circuits. Two requirements constrain any such device: recent inputs must remain
accessible to the readout, and older inputs together with the initial state
must lose their influence. Purely unitary evolution does not by itself supply
this forgetting. Coupling to an environment is therefore not merely an
incidental imperfection in QRC but can be a functional ingredient, and
dissipative dynamics can realize the nonlinear fading-memory maps that the
paradigm requires~\cite{ChenNurdin2019}.

Dissipation is often something to be suppressed, yet engineered environments
have long been used constructively for state preparation, quantum memories,
and computation driven by dissipation itself
~\cite{Poyatos1996,Verstraete2009,Pastawski2011,Harrington2022}. In QRC this
constructive view is well established. Noise, damping, optical absorption, and
measurement back-action can generate or regulate temporal memory
~\cite{Chen2020,Sannia2024,Ricci2026,Gotting2025,Mujal2023,Franceschetto2026},
while feedback, finite-history reinitialization, non-Markovian embeddings, and
optical feedback provide further mechanisms
~\cite{KobayashiFeedback2024,Cindrak2024,SanniaNonMarkov2026,Paparelle2026}.
Studies of coherence and mid-circuit measurement further delimit practically
accessible operating regimes~\cite{Palacios2024,Hu2024}. In particular,
Sannia \emph{et al.} introduced a continuously driven spin reservoir with
uniform local relaxation and showed that its performance improves when the
damping strength is tuned~\cite{Sannia2024}. Related work varies scalar
controls such as the drive, coupling, input duration, and damping
rate~\cite{MartinezPenaPRL2021,Baumann2026}.

A common feature of these continuously driven dissipative QRC studies is that
the dissipator is prescribed and a magnitude attached to it is optimized. A
Markovian generator, however, specifies more than a strength: it also fixes
which combinations of system transitions the environment acts on directly.
Independent losses expose each site separately, whereas a single shared
channel exposes one collective combination and reaches the others indirectly
after coherent dynamics has mixed them into it. That shared decay produces
directly and indirectly coupled sectors is long known from collective emission
and noiseless clusters in quantum networks~\cite{Dicke1954,Cabot2018}.
Whether the organization of that coupling can be harnessed as a computational
design variable in an input-driven reservoir, rather than merely setting a
rate of forgetting, remains open. Continuously driven spin networks with Pauli
readout provide a natural setting in which to address it: the coherent
processor, input encoding, and measured observables can be shared while only
the environmental coupling is changed.

Here we vary the dissipative generator itself rather than only the strength of
a prescribed dissipator. We formulate the design space at the generator level,
separating how strongly the environment acts from which operator combinations
it couples to and how assigned coupling weight is distributed among them.
Comparisons are therefore between generators, not between equivalent
rewritings of a jump-operator list. In the principal paired comparisons,
reservoirs share the Hamiltonian instance, input sequence, observables, readout
protocol, and data splits while coupling organization changes under a fixed
assigned operator-weight budget. The significant advance is computational:
the choice of coupling changes what the readout can still recover about earlier
inputs, and a shared relaxation channel makes more recent input history
accessible than independent local losses. This ordering recurs after long
washout, across the tested finite sizes and spin-Hamiltonian ensembles, and
under reset-based input encoding. It also survives separate comparisons that
match mean expected jump activity or a representative midpoint Liouvillian gap
and a bounded comparison with independently selected operating points. Most
sharply, matched rank-one interventions show that rotating only the coupled
collective direction changes accessible memory while Kossakowski rank,
nonzero spectrum and trace, sitewise diagonal weights, coefficient magnitudes,
and assigned operator weight remain fixed. Equal assigned weight, and even
these matched coarse Kossakowski quantities, therefore does not imply equal
computation. Across the fixed-weight continuously driven benchmarks, different
jump families favor different tasks rather than one being uniformly better;
a finite-sampling analysis identifies when exact-expectation contrasts become
statistically resolvable.

These results bridge the scalar-rate picture of dissipation in QRC and the
broader practice of dissipation engineering, where the system--environment
coupling is itself designed
~\cite{Poyatos1996,Verstraete2009,Pastawski2011,Harrington2022,Cattaneo2019,
Brehm2021,Sheremet2023,CattaneoPRX2023}. They establish that the scalar-rate
description is incomplete: a damping rate, and even a matched set of coarse
Kossakowski quantities, does not determine what a reservoir remembers. Within
the matched rank-one interventions, the supported environmental direction is
therefore a design coordinate that must be chosen and reported rather than left
implicit in a choice of jump operators, and it is a quantity that shared
resonators, waveguides, and structured couplers can directly control.
Controlled forgetting in an open quantum reservoir is not set by one rate
alone: how the processor is coupled to its environment belongs to its
architecture alongside the Hamiltonian and the readout. Here, memory means
previous-input information accessible to the specified Pauli readout after
washout; the finite-reservoir result is neither an asymptotic law nor a unique
mechanism, and fixed assigned weight is not physical equivalence.
