\begin{abstract}
\small
Quantum reservoir computing processes temporal data through fixed quantum
dynamics, but useful operation requires controlled forgetting. In QRC,
engineered dissipation is commonly tuned through an overall rate; here we show
that the generator-level organization of environmental coupling is an
architectural degree of freedom. We characterize this organization by two
operational coordinates: the jump family specifies which combinations of
system operators couple directly to the environment, while the rate profile
specifies how assigned coupling weight is distributed within that family. In
paired comparisons of continuously driven spin reservoirs, we hold fixed the
coherent processor, input, Pauli readout, data splits, and operator-weight
budget. Equal-phase collective relaxation produces a pronounced
short-term-memory advantage over uniform local relaxation. This ordering
recurs in separate comparisons with matched mean expected jump activity, a
matched midpoint Liouvillian gap, bounded independent operating-point
selection, and reset-based encoding. Matched rank-one interventions further
isolate Kossakowski orientation relative to the fixed processor: rotating the
supported collective direction changes accessible memory while Kossakowski
rank, nonzero spectrum and trace, sitewise diagonal weights, coefficient
magnitudes, and assigned operator weight remain fixed. Across the broader
fixed-weight family comparison, no continuously driven design leads every
tested task, so those rankings remain protocol-dependent. The organization of
environmental coupling can thus systematically shape which traces of input
history and which computations remain accessible to the readout.
\end{abstract}
